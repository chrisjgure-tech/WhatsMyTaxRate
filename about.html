How the calculations work

Federal income tax

We apply the 2026 federal tax brackets from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 to your taxable income — that is, gross income minus the standard deduction for your filing status. Each bracket rate applies only to the income that falls within it, not to your total income. The result is your federal income tax; dividing by gross income gives your federal effective rate.

FICA (Social Security + Medicare)

Social Security is taxed at 6.2% on wages up to the SSA wage base ($176,100 for 2026). Medicare is taxed at 1.45% on all wages, with an additional 0.9% on wages above $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly). These rates are the employee share only.

State income tax

State rates are sourced from the Tax Foundation's 2026 state income tax data and verified against each state's department of revenue. We apply the standard deduction (or equivalent) for each state where available. Local and city income taxes are not included; a disclosure note appears for states with significant local taxes (New York City, Maryland counties, Ohio municipalities).

What this tool assumes

  • W-2 wage income only (no self-employment income, capital gains, or investment income)
  • Standard deduction — no itemized deductions unless you use the itemize section
  • No Alternative Minimum Tax, Net Investment Income Tax, or additional Medicare surtax beyond the 0.9%
  • No tax credits beyond those explicitly modeled in the credits section
  • Married filing jointly calculations assume both spouses' income is entered as a combined total

Data sources

  • IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 — 2026 federal tax brackets, standard deductions, and contribution limits
  • Social Security Administration — 2026 Social Security wage base
  • Tax Foundation — 2026 state income tax rates and brackets
  • IRS SOI Historical Table 23 — historical top marginal rates used in the history chart
  • Individual state departments of revenue — verified state standard deductions and local tax disclosures
