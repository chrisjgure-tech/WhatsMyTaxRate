The problem this solves

Every year, millions of Americans quote the wrong tax rate. They land in the 22% federal bracket and assume that's what they pay — but the bracket is the rate on their last dollar, not their overall bill. A $75,000 single filer's real federal effective rate is around 13%. This tool shows the gap, explains why it exists, and breaks down exactly where every dollar goes across federal income tax, FICA, and state tax.

Who built this

What's My Tax Rate? was built by a tax and personal-finance researcher with a background in US tax law and data journalism. The calculator logic, bracket data, and state-rate tables are reviewed against IRS publications and Tax Foundation figures before each filing season to make sure nothing is stale or misleading.

The goal is simple: a working W-2 earner should be able to open this tool, enter their income, and come away understanding what they actually pay — not the shorthand version that shows up in every conversation about taxes.

Methodology

All calculations assume W-2 wage income, the standard deduction, and the 2026 tax year brackets from IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32. Federal effective and marginal rates, FICA (Social Security and Medicare payroll taxes), and state income tax are computed entirely in your browser — the income and contribution figures you enter never leave your device.

State rates and deductions are sourced from the Tax Foundation and verified annually. Local and municipal taxes, the Alternative Minimum Tax, self-employment tax, capital gains rates, and itemized deductions beyond what the tool models are explicitly excluded — the disclaimer at the bottom of the calculator lists every limitation.

What this tool is not

This is an educational tool, not tax software. It does not file your return, prepare any forms, or give personalised advice for your situation. For anything that affects real financial decisions, consult a qualified CPA, enrolled agent, or tax attorney. Individual circumstances — investment income, self-employment, multiple states, deductions — can change the numbers materially.
