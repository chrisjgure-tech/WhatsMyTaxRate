About What's My Tax Rate

A plain-language tool for W-2 earners who want to understand what they actually pay — not just their bracket.

What the tool does

What's My Tax Rate is a free, browser-based calculator that breaks down your federal, FICA, and state income tax for the 2026 tax year. It shows your effective rate — the share of your total income that goes to tax — alongside your marginal rate, so you can see the gap between the two numbers most people confuse.

All calculations run locally in your browser. Your income is never sent to any server, analytics system, or ad platform. The only data that leaves your device is your state and filing status, which are used for privacy-preserving analytics.

Who built it

What's My Tax Rate was built as a public-interest resource to demystify the US federal and state tax system for everyday W-2 employees. The tool is maintained by the team at whatsmytaxrate.com, with calculations verified against official IRS publications each tax year.

The site does not provide personalised tax, legal, or financial advice. It is an educational tool only — see the disclaimer at the bottom of every page.

Data sources and methodology

All figures are sourced directly from official government publications and cross-checked against independent tax research:

  • Federal tax brackets and standard deductions (2026)
    IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 — the official IRS revenue procedure announcing inflation-adjusted amounts for tax year 2026. Covers the seven ordinary-income brackets, the standard deduction by filing status, and relevant phase-out thresholds.
  • FICA rates and wage base
    Social Security Administration — Contribution and Benefit Base. Social Security tax rate is 6.2% on wages up to the annual wage base; Medicare is 1.45% on all wages (plus 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax above applicable thresholds, not modelled here).
  • State income tax rates
    Tax Foundation — State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets. State brackets, standard deductions, and personal exemptions verified against each state's department of revenue for 2026 where available.
  • Historical top federal marginal rates (1955–2026)
    IRS Statistics of Income, Historical Table 23 and the Tax Foundation's historical federal income tax rates series. Top statutory rate on ordinary income; excludes surtaxes and the Net Investment Income Tax.
  • Social Security benefit taxation thresholds
    IRC §86 and SSA benefit taxation guidance. The combined-income formula and the 50%/85% inclusion thresholds are statutory and unchanged since 1993.

What the tool does not cover

To keep results clear and comparable, the calculator assumes W-2 wage income with the standard deduction. It does not model:

  • Local or municipal income taxes
  • The Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)
  • The Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)
  • Capital gains or qualified dividend rates
  • Self-employment tax
  • Itemized deductions (other than in the "Should you itemize?" section)
  • Most credits (a qualitative overview is shown in the Credits section)

Your actual liability will differ. Consult a qualified tax professional before acting on anything here.
