Who built this

What's My Tax Rate was built by a CPA and software engineer who got tired of explaining to friends and colleagues why their marginal bracket wasn't their actual tax rate — and why the number on their paycheck was misleading. The goal: a plain-language tool that shows real numbers, cites its sources, and doesn't require a tax background to understand.

The CPA background covers federal and state income tax compliance, FICA payroll tax, retirement account rules, and the withholding mechanics that make paychecks look worse than your true bill. The engineering side keeps the calculations current each tax year and the site fast and accessible.

This is an independent project — not affiliated with the IRS, any state tax agency, or any tax preparation service.

How the calculator works

Federal income tax

Federal income tax uses the 2026 brackets and standard deduction amounts from IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32. The calculator subtracts the standard deduction from gross income to get taxable income, then runs it through the progressive brackets for the selected filing status. Each bracket's rate applies only to the portion of income within that band — not to all income.

Effective rate = total federal income tax ÷ gross income.
Marginal rate = the bracket containing the last dollar of income.

FICA (Social Security & Medicare)

FICA is applied as two flat rates on gross wages:

  • Social Security: 6.2% on wages up to the annual wage base ($184,500 for 2026, per SSA OACT). Wages above this cap are not subject to Social Security tax.
  • Medicare: 1.45% on all wages, with no cap. (An additional 0.9% applies above $200,000 single / $250,000 married, but the calculator focuses on the base rate for clarity.)

These are the employee-side rates. Employers match them, but the employee portion is what comes out of your paycheck.

State income tax

State rates and brackets are sourced from each state's Department of Revenue (or equivalent), cross-referenced with the Tax Foundation's state income tax rate series. The calculator applies the state's standard deduction or personal exemption where applicable, and uses the filing-status-appropriate bracket schedule. States with no income tax (e.g., Texas, Florida, Nevada) return a $0 state liability.

Local and municipal income taxes are excluded — they vary by city and county and would require a separate data layer. The calculator notes this where relevant.

Withholding estimate

The withholding comparison uses the IRS Publication 15-T (2026) percentage method tables, annualized from a standard biweekly paycheck. It assumes the default W-4 with no adjustments — the result most employees see who haven't updated their W-4 for their specific situation. The purpose is to show why withholding often exceeds your actual tax bill, not to produce a precise withholding amount.

What the calculator excludes

  • Self-employment income and self-employment tax
  • Capital gains and qualified dividends (lower rates apply)
  • Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)
  • Net Investment Income Tax (3.8% on investment income above income thresholds)
  • Itemized deductions (the tool has a separate itemize calculator)
  • Most tax credits (the tool has a separate credits explainer)
  • Local and municipal income taxes

The calculator is designed for W-2 wage earners taking the standard deduction — the most common situation. For other income types, results will not reflect actual liability.

Data sources

Every figure in the calculator is drawn from primary government or non-partisan sources. All figures reflect the 2026 tax year.

Federal tax brackets & standard deduction

  • IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 — the official 2026 inflation adjustments for tax rate schedules, standard deduction amounts, and other parameters.

FICA & Social Security wage base

  • Social Security Administration — Cost-of-Living Adjustments — the annual Social Security wage base announcement.
  • IRS Publication 15 (Employer's Tax Guide) — FICA rates, deposit schedules, and supplemental wage withholding rules.
  • IRS Publication 15-T (Federal Income Tax Withholding Methods) — percentage method withholding tables used for the paycheck estimate.

State income tax rates

  • Each state's Department of Revenue (or equivalent) — primary source for rates, brackets, and standard deductions.
  • Tax Foundation — State Income Tax Rates — used for cross-referencing and confirming rates.

Historical rates

  • IRS Statistics of Income — Historical Table 23 — top statutory federal marginal rates 1955–2026.
  • Tax Foundation — Historical Federal Income Tax Rates — used for cross-referencing the historical rate series.

Last updated: July 2026. State rates and deductions are reviewed at the start of each tax year and updated when a state legislature makes a mid-year change. If you spot an error, please contact us.
