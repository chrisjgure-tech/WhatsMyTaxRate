What this table doesn’t show

State income tax is only one piece of the picture. Here are the most common blind spots.

Local taxes can add 1%–4%

Many states allow cities and counties to levy their own income taxes. Maryland counties add 2.25%–3.2%. Indiana counties add 0.5%–3%. New York City residents owe an extra 3.1%–3.9% on top of New York State. Ohio has hundreds of municipal tax jurisdictions. The table above shows state-level rates only.

Your effective rate is well below the top bracket

In graduated states, only the dollars above each threshold hit the higher rate — so a 9.85% top bracket doesn’t mean paying 9.85% on everything. Your real state effective rate is typically several points below the top marginal rate shown here. Use the calculator to see your actual state effective rate at your income.

No income tax ≠ low state taxes overall

States without an income tax often make up the revenue elsewhere. Texas and Washington have above-average sales tax rates. Alaska relies on oil revenues and a Permanent Fund dividend. Tennessee has higher-than-average sales tax. The total state-and-local tax burden can still be significant even with a 0% income tax rate.

Washington’s capital gains excise tax

Washington has no income tax on wages. But since 2022 it levies a 7% excise tax on long-term capital gains above $270,000. This doesn’t affect most W-2 earners, but it matters if you have large investment gains.
