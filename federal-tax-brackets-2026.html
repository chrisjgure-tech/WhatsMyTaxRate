How to use this table

The key rule: brackets are marginal, not flat

Your tax rate does not apply to all of your income. Each bracket applies only to the slice of income that falls within it. This is what "progressive" means — the higher rates kick in incrementally, not all at once.

Worked example: single filer, $80,000 gross income

Assume the 2026 standard deduction of $16,100 for a single filer. Taxable income is $80,000 − $16,100 = $63,900. Here is how the tax is calculated bracket by bracket:

Bracket Rate Income in bracket Tax owed
$0 – $12,400 10% $12,400 $1,240
$12,401 – $50,400 12% $38,000 $4,560
$50,401 – $63,900 22% $13,500 $2,970
Total federal income tax $8,770

Federal effective rate: $8,770 ÷ $80,000 = 10.96% — far below the 22% marginal rate. The marginal rate is the rate on the last dollar earned, not the average rate on all earnings.
