New York Income Tax Rate (2026): What You Actually Pay

New York State has 9 income tax brackets, with rates from 3.9% to 10.9%. If you live in New York City, you pay additional city income tax on top — one of the highest combined burdens in the country. Here are the 2026 brackets and what they mean for a typical earner.

Key facts: New York income tax 2026

  • Top marginal rate: 10.9% (income above $25 million single)
  • Most common top bracket: 6.85% (income $215,400–$1,077,550)
  • Brackets: 9
  • Standard deduction (single): $8,000
  • Standard deduction (married filing jointly): $16,050
  • Social Security: Fully exempt from New York State income tax
  • NYC residents: Pay additional 3.1%–3.9% city income tax on top of state rates
  • Yonkers residents: Pay a city surcharge in addition to the state rate

Effective rate vs. marginal rate

New York's brackets are progressive — your income fills the lower brackets first. A single filer earning $75,000 in New York State (outside NYC) in 2026:

  • Subtracts the $8,000 standard deduction → $67,000 taxable income
  • Tops out in the 5.4% bracket (not the 6.85% bracket)
  • Pays an estimated New York State effective rate of about 4.6% of gross income

NYC residents add the city tax on top. Use the calculator for exact figures at your income and filing status.

Comparing New York to no-income-tax states

Moving from New York to a state with no income tax — like Texas, Florida, or Nevada — eliminates state income tax entirely. At $100,000 (single filer), the New York state tax savings are roughly $5,300 per year. NYC residents save an additional $3,200+ from the city tax elimination, for a combined savings of ~$8,500 per year.

Frequently asked questions

Does New York tax Social Security?

No. New York State does not tax Social Security benefits. Your Social Security income is fully exempt from New York State income tax regardless of how much you receive or what other income you have. Federal income tax on Social Security still applies based on your combined income.

What is New York's top tax rate?

New York's top marginal state income tax rate is 10.9%, applying to income above $25 million (single). For most high earners, the relevant top rate is 9.65% (income $1,077,550–$5,000,000) or 6.85% (income $215,400–$1,077,550). NYC residents pay an additional city income tax of roughly 3.1%–3.9%, bringing combined rates as high as ~14.8%.

Do New York City residents pay extra income tax?

Yes. NYC residents pay New York City income tax on top of state income tax — roughly 3.1% to 3.9% depending on income. This is a separate city tax, not a state surcharge. Yonkers residents pay a different surcharge. New York State residents who live outside NYC and Yonkers pay only the state rate.

Is New York's income tax higher than California's?

California's top state rate is 13.3% vs. New York's 10.9% at the state level. However, NYC residents face an additional city income tax, making the combined NYC rate (~14.8% at the top) comparable to or higher than California's 13.3%. For most middle-income earners, effective rates in both states are similar — around 4–6% of gross income.
