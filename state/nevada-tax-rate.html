Nevada Income Tax Rate (2026): What You Actually Pay

Nevada has no state income tax on wages — your state income tax rate is 0%. Nevada residents still pay federal income tax and FICA, but the absence of state income tax, combined with Nevada's relatively low cost of living outside Las Vegas, makes it a popular destination for people relocating from California and other high-tax states.

Nevada state income tax: $0

Nevada levies no individual income tax on wages, salaries, interest, dividends, or capital gains. There are no state income tax brackets, no Nevada state income tax filing requirement, and nothing withheld from Nevada paychecks for state income tax.

The Nevada Constitution prohibits a personal income tax. Nevada funds its government primarily through gaming taxes, sales taxes, and room taxes — meaning a significant portion of Nevada's tax burden falls on tourists rather than residents.

What Nevada residents do pay in 2026

  • Federal income tax: The same progressive brackets as every other US state — 10% to 37%
  • Social Security (FICA): 6.2% on wages up to $184,500
  • Medicare (FICA): 1.45% on all wages (0.9% surcharge above $200,000 single)
  • State income tax: $0
  • State sales tax: 6.85% state base rate; local jurisdictions can add up to 1.525% (combined max 8.375% in Clark County)
  • Property tax: Nevada caps residential property tax growth, keeping effective rates modest — typically 0.5%–0.7% of assessed value

The Nevada advantage: income tax savings vs. higher sales tax

Nevada's lack of income tax is partially offset by higher sales taxes — Clark County (Las Vegas) has a combined sales tax rate of about 8.375%, higher than California's 7.25% base rate (though many California localities add more). Nevada's property tax rates are among the lowest in the Western US, helping to offset the sales tax impact for homeowners.

For most wage earners — especially those earning above $75,000 — the absence of state income tax is a larger benefit than the marginal increase in sales tax costs. This trade-off reverses for very low earners who pay little income tax anyway.

Frequently asked questions

Does Nevada have a state income tax?

No. Nevada has no individual state income tax. The Nevada Constitution prohibits a personal income tax. Nevada residents pay $0 in state income tax on wages, investment income, Social Security, or retirement distributions.

Does Nevada tax Social Security or pension income?

No. Because Nevada has no income tax at all, Social Security benefits, pension income, 401(k) and IRA withdrawals, and other retirement distributions are completely free from Nevada state income tax. Federal income tax on Social Security may still apply depending on your combined income.

What taxes do Nevada residents pay?

Nevada residents pay federal income tax and FICA (Social Security + Medicare). Nevada levies a sales tax up to 8.375% combined state and local. There is no state-level income tax, estate tax, or inheritance tax. Property taxes are low — Nevada caps annual increases and effective rates are typically 0.5%–0.7%.

Is Nevada better than Texas for taxes?

Both Nevada and Texas have no state income tax. For most wage earners, the income tax savings are identical. The main difference is in property and sales taxes: Nevada has lower property taxes than Texas (typically 0.5%–0.7% vs. 1.5%–2.5%) but somewhat higher sales taxes in some Nevada jurisdictions. Homeowners often favor Nevada's lower property tax burden.
