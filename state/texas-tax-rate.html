Texas Income Tax Rate (2026): What You Actually Pay

Texas has no state income tax on wages — your state income tax rate is 0%, regardless of how much you earn. That doesn't mean Texans pay no tax; federal income tax and FICA apply just as they do everywhere. Here's the full picture of what Texas residents pay in 2026.

Texas state income tax: $0

Texas levies no individual income tax on wages. There are no brackets, no rates, and no filing requirement for state income tax. A Texas resident earning $30,000 or $3,000,000 in wages pays $0 in Texas state income tax.

The Texas Constitution requires a statewide voter referendum to impose a personal income tax — which has never succeeded — making Texas one of nine states with no wage income tax.

What Texas residents do pay in 2026

  • Federal income tax: The same progressive brackets as every other state — 10% to 37%
  • Social Security (FICA): 6.2% on wages up to $184,500
  • Medicare (FICA): 1.45% on all wages (0.9% surcharge above $200,000 single)
  • State income tax: $0
  • State sales tax: 6.25% state rate; local jurisdictions can add up to 2% (combined max 8.25%)
  • Property tax: Texas has no state property tax, but local rates are among the highest in the US — often 1.5%–2.5% of assessed home value per year

The trade-off: no income tax ≠ low overall tax burden

Texas offsets the lack of income tax with relatively high property and sales taxes. Texas property tax rates are among the highest in the nation — homeowners in many Texas counties pay 1.8%–2.5% of assessed value annually, versus 0.7%–1.0% in California (which has Proposition 13 limits).

For renters or high-income earners with modest property, the absence of state income tax is usually a clear advantage. Homeowners should factor in property tax when comparing the overall state tax burden.

Frequently asked questions

Does Texas have a state income tax?

No. Texas levies no individual income tax on wages. Texas residents pay $0 in state income tax. The Texas Constitution requires a voter-approved referendum to impose one, making it very difficult to add.

What taxes do Texas residents pay?

Texas residents still pay federal income tax (same as all US residents) and FICA payroll tax (Social Security + Medicare). Texas also levies a sales tax up to 8.25% combined state and local, and local property taxes are among the highest in the US. There is no state-level property tax.

Does Texas tax Social Security or pension income?

Texas has no state income tax at all, so Social Security benefits, pension income, and retirement distributions are all free from Texas state tax. Federal income tax rules on Social Security still apply.

How much do Texas residents save compared to California residents?

At $100,000 income (single filer), a Texas resident saves roughly $5,200 in state income tax compared to a California resident, and roughly $5,300 compared to a New York resident. At $200,000, the California gap grows to about $14,000. Higher property taxes in Texas partially offset this advantage for homeowners.
