California Income Tax Rate (2026): What You Actually Pay

California has 10 state income tax brackets, with rates from 1% to 13.3%. The 13.3% top rate — the highest in the nation — only applies above $1 million. Most residents land well below it. Here are the brackets, what they mean for a typical earner, and how California compares.

Key facts: California income tax 2026

  • Top marginal rate: 13.3% (highest in the US)
  • Brackets: 10 (most of any state)
  • Standard deduction (single): $5,706 — lower than the $16,100 federal deduction
  • Standard deduction (married filing jointly): $11,412
  • Social Security: Fully exempt from California income tax
  • HSA contributions: Not deductible; California never adopted federal HSA rules
  • State Disability Insurance (SDI): Roughly 1%+ on wages, no wage ceiling — not shown in brackets above

Effective rate vs. marginal rate: the gap is large

California's brackets are progressive — your income fills the lower brackets first before any reaches the higher ones. A single filer earning $75,000 in 2026:

  • Subtracts the $5,706 standard deduction → $69,294 taxable income
  • Tops out in the 8% bracket, not the 13.3% bracket
  • Pays an estimated California effective rate of about 3.9% of gross income

California vs. no-income-tax states

Nine US states — including Texas, Florida, and Nevada — levy no state income tax on wages. Moving from California to one of them doesn't change your federal or FICA burden, but it eliminates California state income tax entirely. For a $100,000 single earner, that's roughly $4,500–$5,500 per year in state tax savings.

Frequently asked questions

Does California tax Social Security?

No. California does not tax Social Security benefits at the state level. Your benefits are fully exempt from California income tax, regardless of income. They may still be partially taxable at the federal level depending on your combined income — the federal government taxes 0–85% of benefits depending on provisional income.

What is California's top tax rate?

California's top marginal state income tax rate is 13.3% — the highest in the US. This rate applies only to taxable income above $1,000,000 for single filers. It includes the 1% Mental Health Services surtax. Because the system is progressive, reaching this bracket does not mean paying 13.3% on all your income — only on the dollars above $1 million.

Are 401(k) and IRA contributions deductible in California?

Traditional 401(k) and most employer-plan deferrals are deductible for California income tax. However, HSA contributions are not deductible in California — the state never conformed to federal HSA rules, so HSA contributions are treated as taxable California wages and account earnings are taxed annually.

How does California compare to other high-tax states?

California's 13.3% top rate is the nation's highest. New York's top rate is 10.9%; Hawaii's is 11%. For a $75,000 single filer, estimated effective state income tax rates are roughly: California 3.9%, New York 4.6%, Texas 0%, Florida 0%. The effective rate is what you actually pay — always much lower than the top bracket.
